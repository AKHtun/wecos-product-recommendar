## 6. Special-Case Warnings

> **Always purge out the old grease before changing products.** Mixing two greases — even when the generic compatibility chart rates them as Compatible — risks thickener antagonism, additive antagonism, softening / hardening, or oil bleed. ASTM D6185 confirms that the structural-stability compatibility chart does NOT predict additive chemistry, seal compatibility, or long-term performance. The safest practice is to fully clean and regrease the bearings, or thoroughly purge the old grease with the new one, increasing relubrication frequency until clean product exits the relief points.

> **Avoid over-greasing — it is the most frequent cause of bearing failure.** Always have relief plugs removed during the first hour of operation after regreasing to allow excess grease to flow out freely. Monitor temperature, vibration, and visual leaks for the first 24 hours.

> **Base-oil viscosity and NLGI consistency are NOT the same.** Base-oil viscosity (ISO VG) is the property that lubricates; NLGI consistency is how stiff the finished grease is. The most common grease-selection mistake is to substitute one for the other. Confirm both before signing off.
